Section 4.4 Coordinate System

Theorem 8 The Unique Representation Theorem

Let B={b1,…,bn}{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_1,\dots, b_n\}B={b1​,…,bn​} be a basis for a vector space VVV. Then for each xxx in VVV, there exists a unique set of scalars c1,…,cnc_1,\dots, c_nc1​,…,cn​ such that

x=c1b1+⋯+cnbnx=c_1b_1+\cdots + c_nb_n x=c1​b1​+⋯+cn​bn​

Definition

Suppose B={b1,…,bn}{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_1,\dots, b_n\}B={b1​,…,bn​} is a basis for a vector space VVV and xxx is in VVV. The coordinates of xxx relative to the basis B{\mathcal{B}}B (or the B{\mathcal{B}}B - coordinates of xxx ) are the weights c1,…,cnc_1,\dots,c_nc1​,…,cn​ such that x=c1b1+⋯+cnbnx=c_1b_1+\dots + c_n b_nx=c1​b1​+⋯+cn​bn​.

Let the change-of-coordinates matrix PBP_BPB​ be:

PB=[b1  b2⋯bn]P_{\mathcal{B}}=[b_1\; b_2\cdots b_n] PB​=[b1​b2​⋯bn​]

Then the vector equation

x=c1b1+c2b2+⋯+cnbnx=c_1b_1+c_2b_2+\cdots + c_nb_n x=c1​b1​+c2​b2​+⋯+cn​bn​

is equivalent to

x=PB[x]B\boxed{x=P_{\mathcal{B}}[x]_{\mathcal{B}}} x=PB​[x]B​​

Hence:

PB−1x=[x]BP_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}x=[x]_{\mathcal{B}} PB−1​x=[x]B​

Theorem 9

Let B={b1,…,bn}B=\{b_1,\dots, b_n\}B={b1​,…,bn​} be a basis for a vector space VVV. Then the coordinate mapping x↦[x]B\mathbf{x} \mapsto [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}x↦[x]B​ is a one-to-one linear transformation from VVV onto Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn.

A one-to-one linear transformation from a vector space VVV onto a vector space WWW is called an isomorphism from VVV onto WWW.

Section 4.5 The Dimension of a Vector Space

Theorem 10

If a vector space VVV has a basis B={b1,…,bn}B=\{b_1,\dots,b_n\}B={b1​,…,bn​}, then any set in VVV containing more than nnn vectors must be linearly dependent.

Theorem 11

If a vector space VVV has a basis of nnn vectors, then every basis of VVV must consist of exactly nnn vectors.

Definition

If a vector space VVV is spanned by a finite set, then VVV is said to be finite-dimensional and the dimension of VVV, written as dim VVV, is the number of vectors in a basis for VVV. The dimension of the zero vector space {0}\{0\}{0} is defined to be zero. If VVV is not spanned by a finite set, then VVV is said to be infinite-dimensional.

Subspace of a Finite-Dimensional Space

Theorem 12

Let HHH be a subspace of a finite-dimensional vector space VVV. Any linearly independent set in HHH can be expanded, if necessary, to a basis for HHH. Also, HHH is finite-dimensional and

dim H≤dim V\text{dim}\,H\le \text{dim}\, V dimH≤dimV

Theorem 13 The Basis Theorem

Let VVV be a p-dimensional vector space, p≥1p\ge 1p≥1. Any linearly independent set of exactly ppp elements in VVV is automatically a basis for VVV. Any set of exactly ppp elements that spans VVV is automatically a basis for VVV.

The dimensions of Nul A, Col A, and Row A

Definition

The rank of an m×nm\times nm×n matrix AAA is the dimension of the column space and the nullity of AAA is the dimension of the null space.

Theorem 14 The Rank Theorem

The dimensions of the column space and the null space of an m×nm\times nm×n matrix AAA satisfy the equation

rank A+nullity A=number of columns in  A\text{rank}\,A+\text{nullity}\, A=\text{number of columns in }\, A rankA+nullityA=number of columns in A

The rank of an m×nm\times nm×n matrix AAA is the number of pivot columns.

The nullity of AAA is the number of free variables.

Since the dimension of row is the number of pivot rows, it is also equal to the rank of AAA.

Theorem The Invertible Matrix Theorem (continued)

  1. The columns of AAA form a basis of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn
  2. Col A=Rn\text{Col}\,A=\mathbb{R}^nColA=Rn
  3. rank A=n\text{rank}\,A=nrankA=n
  4. nullity A=0\text{nullity}\,A=0nullityA=0
  5. Nul A={0}\text{Nul}\,A=\{0\}NulA={0}
