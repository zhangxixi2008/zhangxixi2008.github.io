Honors Physics I - 3 Momentum and Kinetic Energy

Center of Mass (com)

The motion of the mass point is simple. And all other points rotate around this point.

xcom=m2m1+m2d=m1x1+m2x2m1+m2x_{com}=\frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2}d=\frac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2}{m_1+m_2} xcom​=m1​+m2​m2​​d=m1​+m2​m1​x1​+m2​x2​​

For many particles and in higher dimensions :

r⃗com=1M∑i=1nmiri⃗\vec r_{com}=\frac 1 M \sum_{i=1}^nm_i\vec{r_i} rcom​=M1​i=1∑n​mi​ri​​

Usually, the ∑i=1nmiri⃗\sum_{i=1}^nm_i\vec{r_i}∑i=1n​mi​ri​​ is called the moment

For solid bodies:

xcom=1M∫x dmx_{com}=\frac 1 M\int x\, dm xcom​=M1​∫xdm

Or choose an origin, and then:

r⃗com=1M∫r⃗i dx\vec r_{com}=\frac 1 M\int \vec r_i\, dx rcom​=M1​∫ri​dx

If we limit ourselves to uniform density.

ρ=MVxcom=1V∫x dV\rho=\frac M V\\ x_{com}=\frac 1 V\int x\, dV ρ=VM​xcom​=V1​∫xdV

Remember when add or remove things, the total mass will be changed

Newton’s Second Law for a System of Particles

Center of mass motion continues unaffected by forces internal to a system.

Because of the Newton’s third law, the internal force push to each internal object can be eliminated, only the external forces will be remained

F⃗net=Ma⃗net=Mr⃗¨\vec F_{net}=M\vec a_{net}=M\ddot{\vec r} Fnet​=Manet​=Mr¨

Kinetic Energy

Energy

  • A scalar quantity
  • Conserve in a closed system

Kinetic Energy

K=12mv2K=\frac 1 2 mv^2 K=21​mv2

The unit of kinetic energy is a joule (J) 1J=1kg⋅m2/s21J=1kg\cdot m^2/s^21J=1kg⋅m2/s2

Work WWW is energy transferred. Transfer to the object is positive work, while transferred from the object is negative work.

12mv2−12mv02=FxdW=Fxd\frac 1 2mv^2-\frac 1 2mv_0^2=F_xd\\ W=F_xd 21​mv2−21​mv02​=Fx​dW=Fx​d

W=Fdcos⁡ϕW=F⃗⋅d⃗W=Fd\cos \phi\\ W=\vec F\cdot \vec d W=FdcosϕW=F⋅d

  • Relaxed state : no force is applied
  • Stretch and extend the spring it resists : restoring force

Hooke’s law : F⃗s=−kd⃗\vec F_s=-k\vec dFs​=−kd, where kkk is the spring constant.

Ws=∫xixfFxdx=(−12k)(xf2−xi2)=12kxi2−12kxf2W_s=\int_{x_i}^{x_f}F_xdx=(-\frac 1 2k)(x_f^2-x_i^2)=\frac 1 2k x_i^2-\frac 1 2 kx_f^2 Ws​=∫xi​xf​​Fx​dx=(−21​k)(xf2​−xi2​)=21​kxi2​−21​kxf2​

Collision and Impulse

J⃗=∫titfF⃗(t)dt\vec J=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\vec F(t)dt J=∫ti​tf​​F(t)dt

Δp⃗=J⃗\Delta \vec p=\vec J Δp​=J

If no net external force, the P⃗\vec PP is a constant. This is called the law of conservation of linear momentum.

Momentum and Kinetic Energy in Collisions

  • Elastic collisions
    • Total kinetic energy is unchanged
  • Inelastic collisions : some energy is transferred
  • Completely inelastic collisions
    • The objects stick together
    • Greatest loss of kinetic energy
