Sequences

An infinite sequence {xn}\{x_n\}{xn​} is converge to xxx (xxx is called the limit)iff:

∀ε>0,  ∃N    that  ∀n≥N,∣xn−x∣<ε\forall \varepsilon > 0,\;\exist N\;\;\text{that}\;\forall n\ge N,|x_n-x|<\varepsilon ∀ε>0,∃Nthat∀n≥N,∣xn​−x∣<ε

If not, it is called divergent.

Above is called the ε−N\varepsilon -Nε−N definition. (which can also use to prove xn{x_n}xn​ is converge to NNN)

Monotone Convergence Theorem : A monotonic sequences converges iff it is bounded

Subsequences

Omit some terms from a sequence {xn}\{x_n\}{xn​}, and we get a subsequence {xnk}\{x_{n_k}\}{xnk​​}, which is also infinite.

  • Convergence of subsequence : If {xn}\{x_n\}{xn​} converges to xxx, then every {xnk}\{x_{n_k}\}{xnk​​} also converges to xxx
  • Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem : Every bounded sequence has at least one convergent subsequence.

Cauchy Criterion

A sequence {xn}\{x_n\}{xn​}converge iff :
