Sound Waves

Sound waves are longitudinal mechanical waves.

The seed vvv of a sound wave in medium having bulk modulus BBB and density $\rho $ is

v=Bρv=\sqrt{B\over \rho} v=ρB​​

A sound wave causes a longitudinal displacement sss of a mass element in a medium as given by:

s=smcos⁡(kx−ωt)s=s_m\cos(kx-\omega t) s=sm​cos(kx−ωt)

where sms_msm​ is the displacement amplitude.

The wave also causes a pressure change Δp\Delta pΔp from the equilibrium pressure:

Δp=Δpmsin⁡(kx−ωt)\Delta p =\Delta p_m\sin(kx-\omega t) Δp=Δpm​sin(kx−ωt)

where the pressure amplitude is

Δpm=(vρω)sm\Delta p_m=(v\rho \omega )s_m Δpm​=(vρω)sm​

Interference

The interference of two sound waves with identical wavelengths passing through a common point depends on their phase difference ϕ\phiϕ there. If the sound waves were emitted in phase and are traveling in approximately the same direction, ϕ\phiϕ is given by:

ϕ=ΔLλ2π\phi = \frac {\Delta L}{\lambda} 2\pi ϕ=λΔL​2π

where ΔL\Delta LΔL is their path length difference

Fully constructive interference occurs when ϕ\phiϕ is an integer multiple of 2π2\pi2π : ϕ=m(2π)\phi = m(2\pi)ϕ=m(2π), and equivalently, when ΔL\Delta LΔL is related to wavelength λ\lambdaλ by : $\dfrac{\Delta L }\lambda=0,1,2,\dots $

Fully destructive interference occurs when ϕ\phiϕ is an odd multiple of π\piπ, like ϕ=(2m+1)π\phi=(2m+1)\piϕ=(2m+1)π

Sound Intensity

The intensity III of a sound wave at a surface is the average rate per unit area at which energy is transferred by the wave through or onto the surface:

I=PAI=\frac P A I=AP​

where PPP is the time rate of energy transfer (power) of the sound wave and AAA is the area of the surface intercepting the sound.

I=12ρvω2sm2I=\frac 1 2 \rho v\omega ^2s_m^2 I=21​ρvω2sm2​

The intensity at a distance rrr from a point source that emits sound waves of power PsP_sPs​ is

I=Ps4πr2I=\frac{P_s}{4\pi r^2} I=4πr2Ps​​

Standing Wave Patterns in Pipes

Standing sound wave patterns can be set up in pips. A pipe open at both ends will resonate at frequencies:

f=vλ=nv2L,      n=1,2,3,…f=\frac v \lambda = \frac{nv}{2L},\;\;\; n=1,2,3,\dots f=λv​=2Lnv​,n=1,2,3,…

where vvv is the speed of sound in the air in the pipe.

For a pipe closed at one end and open at another, the resonant frequency is

Beats

Beats arise when two waves having slightly different frequencies f1f_1f1​ and f2f_2f2​, are detected together. The beat frequency is

fbeat=f1−f2f_{beat}=f_1-f_2 fbeat​=f1​−f2​

The Doppler Effect

When the source or the detector moves relative to the transmitting medium.

f′=fv±vDv±vSf'=f\frac {v\pm v_D}{v\pm v_S} f′=fv±vS​v±vD​​

  • vDv_DvD​ is the speed of the detector relative to the medium
  • vSv_SvS​ it that of the source
  • vvv is the speed of sound in the medium

Shock Wave

If the speed of a source relative to the medium exceeds the speed of sound in the medium, the Doppler equation no longer applies. In such a case, shock waves result. The half-angle θ\thetaθ of the Mach cone is given by
