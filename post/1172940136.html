Section 8 Degree of Freedom and Molar Specific Heats

Theorem: Equipartition of Energy

Every kind of molecule has a certain number fff of degrees of freedom, which are independent ways in which the molecule stores energy.

For a degree of freedom : an average energy is 12RT\dfrac 1 2RT21​RT, so Eint=f2nRTE_{int}=\dfrac f 2nRTEint​=2f​nRT.

As ΔE=nCVT\Delta E=nC_VTΔE=nCV​T, so: C=f2RC=\dfrac f 2RC=2f​R

Because rotational and oscillatory motions begin at certain energies, only translation is possible at very low temperatures.

As the temperature increases, rotational motion can begin.

At still higher temperatures, oscillatory motion can begin
