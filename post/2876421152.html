Honors Physics Chapter - 16 Waves I

Types of Waves:

  • Mechanical Waves
  • Electromagnetic Waves
  • Matter Waves

Transverse and Longitudinal Waves

  • Transverse Wave: A sinusoidal wave is sent along the string. A typical string element moves up and down continuously as the waves passes.

  • Longitudinal Wave: A sound wave is set up in a air-filled pipe by moving a piston back and forth. Because the oscillations of an element of the air are parallel to the direction in which the wave travels.

Sinusoidal Waves

y(x,t)=ymsin⁡(kx−ωt)y(x,t)=y_m\sin(kx-\omega t) y(x,t)=ym​sin(kx−ωt)

  • kkk : Angular wave number (rad/m)

Interference of Waves

Superposition of Waves

When two or more waves traverse the same medium, the displacement of any particle of the medium is the sum of the displacements that the individual waves would give it.

If two are travelling in the same direction and have the same amplitude ymy_mym​ and frequency but differ in phase by a phase constant $\phi $:

y′(x,t)=[2ymcos⁡(12ϕ)]sin⁡(kx−ωt+12ϕ)y'(x,t)=[2y_m\cos(\frac 1 2\phi)]\sin(kx-\omega t +\frac 1 2\phi) y′(x,t)=[2ym​cos(21​ϕ)]sin(kx−ωt+21​ϕ)

  • ϕ=0\phi = 0ϕ=0, this is a fully constructive interference
  • ϕ=π rad\phi =\pi\, \text{rad}ϕ=πrad, this is a fully destructive interfrence

Phasors

A wave y(x,t)y(x,t)y(x,t) can be represented with a phasor:

  1. Phasor is a vector
  2. Its magnitude equal to the amplitude ymy_mym​ of the wave
  3. It rotates about an origin with an angular speed equal to angular frequency ω\omegaω
  4. The projection of the rotating phasor on a vertical axis gives the displacement yyy
