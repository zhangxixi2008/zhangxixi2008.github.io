Chapter 12: Equilibrium and Elasticity

Part I: Static Equilibrium

1. Conditions for Static Equilibrium

A body is in static equilibrium when it remains at rest, with zero net external force and zero net external torque.

For a planar problem in the xyxyxy-plane:

∑Fx=0,∑Fy=0,∑τz=0.\begin{aligned} \sum F_x &= 0, \\ \sum F_y &= 0, \\ \sum \tau_z &= 0. \end{aligned} ∑Fx​∑Fy​∑τz​​=0,=0,=0.​

The first two equations ensure translational equilibrium; the third ensures rotational equilibrium. Static equilibrium additionally requires that the body initially be at rest.

Torque about a chosen pivot is

τ=r×F,τz=xFy−yFx.\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}, \qquad \tau_z = xF_y - yF_x. τ=r×F,τz​=xFy​−yFx​.

Here, r\mathbf{r}r is measured from the pivot to the point where the force acts. A convenient sign convention takes counterclockwise torque as positive and clockwise torque as negative.

Choose a convenient reference level, such as yi=0y_i = 0yi​=0, and assign the potential energy there to be zero. Only potential-energy differences affect the motion.

For a conservative force:

where WconsW_{\mathrm{cons}}Wcons​ is the work done by that conservative force.

3. Procedure for Extended Equilibrium Problems

  1. Draw a free-body diagram. Isolate the body and show all external forces, their points of application, and their assumed directions.
  2. Choose axes and a pivot. State the positive directions. Choosing a pivot through an unknown force’s line of action eliminates its torque from the torque equation.
  3. Apply the equilibrium equations. Write ∑Fx=0\sum F_x = 0∑Fx​=0, ∑Fy=0\sum F_y = 0∑Fy​=0, and ∑τz=0\sum \tau_z = 0∑τz​=0.
  4. Solve the algebra. Use consistent units and perpendicular lever arms.
  5. Check signs and directions. A negative answer means the actual direction is opposite to the assumed direction.

Part II: Elasticity

1. Stress, Strain, and Elastic Moduli

Within the linear elastic regime:

stress=elastic modulus×strain.\text{stress} = \text{elastic modulus} \times \text{strain}. stress=elastic modulus×strain.

  • Stress measures force per unit area and has SI units of pascals: 1 Pa=1 N/m21\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1\,\mathrm{N/m^2}1Pa=1N/m2.
  • Strain measures relative deformation and is dimensionless.
  • Elastic modulus measures resistance to deformation and has units of pascals.

The relations below assume small deformations within the material’s linear elastic range.

For a wire or rod stretched by an axial tensile force FFF:

Therefore,

FA=EΔLL0,ΔL=FL0EA.\frac{F}{A} = E\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}, \qquad \Delta L = \frac{FL_0}{EA}. AF​=EL0​ΔL​,ΔL=EAFL0​​.

Chapter 13: Gravitation

Part I: Gravitational Force

1. Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation

Two point masses attract each other with a force of magnitude

F=Gm1m2r2,F = G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}, F=Gr2m1​m2​​,

where GGG is the universal gravitational constant and rrr is the distance between the masses.

For a source mass MMM at the origin and a test mass mmm at position r\mathbf{r}r:

F=−GMmr2r^.\mathbf{F} = -\frac{GMm}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}. F=−r2GMm​r^.

The unit vector r^\hat{\mathbf{r}}r^ points radially outward, so the minus sign indicates attraction toward MMM.

2. Shell Theorem

For a uniform spherical shell:

Location of the test mass Gravitational effect of the shell
Outside the shell The shell acts as if all its mass were concentrated at its center.
Inside the shell The net gravitational force exerted by the shell is zero.

These results allow a spherically symmetric body to be analyzed as a collection of concentric shells.

Outside a spherically symmetric body

For total mass MMM and outer radius RRR, the external force is

F(r)=−GMmr2r^,r≥R.\mathbf{F}(r) = -\frac{GMm}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}, \qquad r \ge R. F(r)=−r2GMm​r^,r≥R.

Inside a uniform solid sphere

For a solid sphere of uniform density, only the mass enclosed within radius rrr contributes to the net force. Shells outside that radius contribute zero net force.

Menc(r)=Mr3R3.M_{\mathrm{enc}}(r) = M\frac{r^3}{R^3}. Menc​(r)=MR3r3​.

Therefore,

F(r)=−GmMenc(r)r2r^=−GMmR3r,0<r≤R.\mathbf{F}(r) = -\frac{GmM_{\mathrm{enc}}(r)}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}} = -\frac{GMm}{R^3}\mathbf{r}, \qquad 0 < r \le R. F(r)=−r2GmMenc​(r)​r^=−R3GMm​r,0<r≤R.

At the center, the net gravitational force is zero.

The force magnitude is proportional to the distance from the center:

F(r)=GMmR3r=GMmR2rR.F(r) = \frac{GMm}{R^3}r = \frac{GMm}{R^2}\frac{r}{R}. F(r)=R3GMm​r=R2GMm​Rr​.

Clarification: Zero force applies inside a spherical shell. Inside a uniform solid sphere, the force is generally nonzero and varies linearly with rrr.

3. Superposition

Gravitational forces add as vectors.

For discrete masses:

Fnet=∑iFi.\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{net}} = \sum_i \mathbf{F}_i. Fnet​=i∑​Fi​.

For a continuous mass distribution:

Fnet=∫dF.\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{net}} = \int d\mathbf{F}. Fnet​=∫dF.

Resolve the contributions into components before adding them when their directions differ.

4. Gravitational Acceleration and Apparent Weight

Gravitational acceleration

The gravitational force determines the gravitational acceleration g\mathbf{g}g:

Fg=mg.\mathbf{F}_g = m\mathbf{g}. Fg​=mg.

Outside a spherically symmetric body:

g(r)=−GMr2r^,g(r)=GMr2.\mathbf{g}(r) = -\frac{GM}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}, \qquad g(r) = \frac{GM}{r^2}. g(r)=−r2GM​r^,g(r)=r2GM​.

At the surface, r=Rr = Rr=R, so

g=GMR2.g = \frac{GM}{R^2}. g=R2GM​.

Effect of rotation at the equator

Consider an object stationary relative to the equator of a rotating spherical planet. Let NNN be the normal force, ω\omegaω the planet’s angular speed, and outward be positive.

The object has inward centripetal acceleration, so

N−mg=−mω2R.N - mg = -m\omega^2R. N−mg=−mω2R.

Thus, the apparent weight is

N=m(g−ω2R).N = m\left(g - \omega^2R\right). N=m(g−ω2R).

The apparent gravitational acceleration at the equator is therefore

gapp=Nm=g−ω2R.g_{\mathrm{app}} = \frac{N}{m} = g - \omega^2R. gapp​=mN​=g−ω2R.

This expression assumes an ideal spherical planet and applies at the equator.

Part II: Gravitational Potential Energy

1. Potential Energy and Its Reference Level

For two point masses, or a test mass outside a spherically symmetric body, choose U(∞)=0U(\infty) = 0U(∞)=0. Then

U(r)=−GMmr.U(r) = -\frac{GMm}{r}. U(r)=−rGMm​.

  • As r→∞r \to \inftyr→∞, U(r)→0U(r) \to 0U(r)→0 from below.
  • Increasing the separation increases the potential energy.

The radial force component is

Fr=−dUdr=−GMmr2.F_r = -\frac{dU}{dr} = -\frac{GMm}{r^2}. Fr​=−drdU​=−r2GMm​.

The negative sign indicates that the force points toward decreasing rrr. The force magnitude is GMm/r2GMm/r^2GMm/r2.

3. Conservation of Mechanical Energy

When gravity is the only force doing work:

E=K+U=constant.E = K + U = \text{constant}. E=K+U=constant.

For motion in the gravitational field of a fixed source mass MMM:

12mvi2−GMmri=12mvf2−GMmrf.\frac{1}{2}mv_i^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_i} = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_f}. 21​mvi2​−ri​GMm​=21​mvf2​−rf​GMm​.

Here, vvv is measured in an inertial frame in which the source mass is at rest. The fixed-source approximation is appropriate when M≫mM \gg mM≫m.

4. Escape Speed

Escape speed is the minimum initial speed needed to reach infinity with zero final speed, assuming no further propulsion or energy loss.

For this limiting case:

r→∞,U→0,K→0.r \to \infty, \qquad U \to 0, \qquad K \to 0. r→∞,U→0,K→0.

The total mechanical energy is therefore zero:

12mvesc2−GMmr0=0.\frac{1}{2}mv_{\mathrm{esc}}^2 - \frac{GMm}{r_0} = 0. 21​mvesc2​−r0​GMm​=0.

Solving gives

vesc=2GMr0.v_{\mathrm{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r_0}}. vesc​=r0​2GM​​.

For launch from the surface, r0=Rr_0 = Rr0​=R:

vesc=2GMR=2gR.v_{\mathrm{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR}. vesc​=R2GM​​=2gR​.

The escape speed is independent of the escaping object’s mass. The surface formula neglects atmospheric drag and planetary rotation.

Part III: Kepler’s Laws

1. First Law: Law of Orbits

A planet in a bound Keplerian orbit moves along an ellipse with the Sun at one focus.

  • The semimajor axis aaa specifies the size of the ellipse.
  • A circular orbit is a special case with constant orbital radius r=ar = ar=a.

2. Second Law: Law of Areas

The line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times.

dAdt=constant.\frac{dA}{dt} = \text{constant}. dtdA​=constant.

Gravity is a central force, so its torque about the attracting center is zero. Angular momentum is therefore conserved:

L=mr2θ˙=constant,dAdt=L2m.L = mr^2\dot{\theta} = \text{constant}, \qquad \frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{L}{2m}. L=mr2θ˙=constant,dtdA​=2mL​.

Consequently, a planet moves fastest at its closest point to the Sun and slowest at its farthest point.

3. Third Law: Law of Periods

For an object orbiting a dominant central mass MMM:

T2=4π2GMa3.T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3. T2=GM4π2​a3.

Here, TTT is the orbital period and aaa is the semimajor axis.

For objects orbiting the same central mass:

T2a3=constant.\frac{T^2}{a^3} = \text{constant}. a3T2​=constant.

For a circular orbit, a=ra = ra=r, so

T2=4π2GMr3.T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3. T2=GM4π2​r3.

Clarification: Use the semimajor axis aaa for an elliptical orbit. The orbital radius rrr can replace aaa only for a circular orbit.

For an isolated two-body system without the approximation M≫mM \gg mM≫m, the exact relation is

T2=4π2G(M+m)arel3,T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{G(M+m)}a_{\mathrm{rel}}^3, T2=G(M+m)4π2​arel3​,

where arela_{\mathrm{rel}}arel​ is the semimajor axis of the relative orbit describing the separation between the two bodies.

A torsion pendulum experiences a restoring torque proportional to its angular displacement:

A simple pendulum consists of a point mass mmm suspended from a fixed pivot by a massless, inextensible string of length ℓ\ellℓ.

Chapter 17: Waves II

1. Sound Waves

1.1 Nature and Speed of Sound

Sound in a fluid is a longitudinal mechanical wave: particles oscillate parallel to the direction of propagation, producing alternating compressions and rarefactions.

For small disturbances in a fluid, the sound speed is

v=Bρ,v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}}, v=ρB​​,

where BBB is the appropriate bulk modulus and ρ\rhoρ is the equilibrium mass density. For ordinary sound propagation in a gas, use the adiabatic bulk modulus.

The wave parameters satisfy

v=ωk=λf,k=2πλ,ω=2πf.v = \frac{\omega}{k} = \lambda f, \qquad k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}, \qquad \omega = 2\pi f. v=kω​=λf,k=λ2π​,ω=2πf.

1.2 Displacement and Pressure Variations

Δp(x,t)=−B∂s∂x.\Delta p(x,t) = -B\frac{\partial s}{\partial x}. Δp(x,t)=−B∂x∂s​.
