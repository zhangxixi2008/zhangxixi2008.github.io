Honors Physics Chapter 12 Equilibrium and Elasticity

Equilibrium

LLL is a constant and PPP is a constant.

static equilibrium : at rest

  • F⃗net=dP⃗dt=0\vec F_{\text{net}} = \dfrac {d\vec P}{dt}=0Fnet​=dtdP​=0
  • τ⃗net=dL⃗dt=0\vec \tau_{\text{net}} = \dfrac{d\vec L}{dt} =0τnet​=dtdL​=0

We often simplify matters by considering forces only in the xy plane, so we get:

F⃗net,x=0F⃗net,y=0τ⃗net,z=0\vec F_{\text{net},x} =0\\ \vec F_{\text{net},y} =0\\ \vec \tau_{\text{net},z} =0 Fnet,x​=0Fnet,y​=0τnet,z​=0

3 types of static equilibrium:

  • neutral equilibrium
  • unstable equilibrium
  • stable equilibrium

center of gravity

The net effect may be imagining an equivalent total gravitational force acting at the center of gravity.

If the ggg is the same for all the elements of the body, the center of gravity is at the center of mass.

Elasticity

There are three elastic moduli to describe the elastic deformations

  1. Strain (fractional change in length)
  2. Stress (force per unit area)
  3. Modulus

stress=modulus×strain\text{stress}=\text{modulus}\times \text{strain} stress=modulus×strain

A stress, deforming force per unit area, produces a strain, or unit deformation.

As stress increases, eventually a yield strength is reached and the material deforms permanently.

At the ultimate strength, the material breaks.

Tension and Compression

Young’s modulus:

FA=EΔLL\frac F A = E\frac {\Delta L}L AF​=ELΔL​

Distinguish Compressive strength and tensile strength

Shearing

Shear modulus:

FA=GΔxL\frac F A = G \frac{\Delta x}L AF​=GLΔx​

Hydraulic Stress

Bulk modulus

p=BΔVVp=B\frac {\Delta V}V p=BVΔV​
