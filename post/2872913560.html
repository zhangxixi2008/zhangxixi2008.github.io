Gyroscope

1. Premise and Key Insight

Premise:

  • The angular velocity ω\omegaω is sufficiently large.
  • The spin angular momentum dominates: Lwheel≫LzL_{\text{wheel}} \gg L_zLwheel​≫Lz​.

Key Insight:
The torque τ⃗\vec{\tau}τ only affects the direction of the angular momentum L⃗\vec{L}L, but it does not affect its magnitude.

2. Mathematical Derivation

The magnitude of the torque is given by:

τ=Mgr\tau = Mgr τ=Mgr

The magnitude of the angular momentum is:

L=IωL = I\omega L=Iω

According to the rotational analog of Newton’s second law:

τ⃗=dL⃗dt\vec{\tau} = \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} τ=dtdL​

This implies that the change in angular momentum over a small time interval dtdtdt is:

dL⃗=Mgr dtd\vec{L} = Mgr \, dt dL=Mgrdt

Since the torque is perpendicular to the angular momentum, it causes the angular momentum vector to rotate by an infinitesimal angle dϕd\phidϕ without changing its magnitude. From the vector triangle formed by L⃗\vec{L}L and dL⃗d\vec{L}dL:

dϕ=dLLd\phi = \frac{dL}{L} dϕ=LdL​

Substituting the expressions for dLdLdL and LLL:

dϕ=MgrIωdtd\phi = \frac{Mgr}{I\omega} dt dϕ=IωMgr​dt

3. Precession Rate

The precession rate (angular velocity of precession), denoted by Ω\OmegaΩ, is the rate of change of the angle dϕd\phidϕ with respect to time:

Ω=MgrIω(precession rate)\Omega = \frac{Mgr}{I\omega} \quad \text{(precession rate)} Ω=IωMgr​(precession rate)

Important Conditions and Remarks

  1. ω\omegaω must be sufficiently large.
  2. Ω\OmegaΩ is independent of mass MMM. This is because the moment of inertia III is also proportional to the mass, so the mass terms cancel out in the numerator and denominator.
